Wall unit designs for the living room

Framework Designs, Nairobi 8 September 2026

In a Nairobi living room, the wall unit is the piece around the TV. It holds the TV and the decoder, the speakers, the books and the things you keep on show. It is often the largest piece of furniture in the room, and one of the hardest to get a clear price for.

This is how to plan one, with three layouts and what each costs.

What does a wall unit have to hold?

Start with a list, not a picture. The TV, and whether it stands on its feet or hangs on the wall. The decoder, the router and any speakers, with their cables. Books, if you have them. Photographs, plants and the pieces you want seen.

Then decide what should be on show and what should not. That settles most of the design.

How wide for your wall and your TV?

Measure the wall, then the TV. A TV is sold by the diagonal of its screen, not its width.

Roughly, a 43-inch screen is about 95 cm wide, a 55-inch about 122 cm and a 65-inch about 144 cm. These are approximate, so check your TV's own specification. Our post on how wide a TV stand should be has the full list.

Most of our wall units are about 2 m or 3.00 m long. Leave a little wall showing at each end, so the unit reads as furniture and not as a built-in.

Open shelves or doors?

Doors hide dust and clutter, and open shelves do not. Plan for that. Our units have no back, so cables drop down behind them instead of through holes. Baskets on the lower shelves take the remotes, chargers and papers. The decoder stays in the open, where the remote can reach it.

What open shelves give back is light. A wall of open steel shelving looks lighter than a cabinet of the same size.
